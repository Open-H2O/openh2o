From a zone’s ceiling to an account’s allocation

Topic How it works
Ceilings An administrator or operator enters each allocation ceiling on {% if 'accounting' in enabled_modules %}Allocations{% else %}Allocations{% endif %}, for one zone, one water type and one water year.
An account’s share OpenH2O shares a zone’s groundwater ceiling equally among the zone’s use areas. An account’s allocation in the zone is the ceiling times the number of the account’s use areas in the zone, divided by the number of use areas in the zone.
Why use areas are counted OpenH2O counts use areas because acreage may be missing from parcel records.
More than one zone An account with use areas in several zones receives the sum of its shares from each zone.
Surface water ceilings OpenH2O leaves a surface water ceiling undivided. On the zone’s page, OpenH2O compares it with the deliveries recorded on the zone’s use areas for the water year.

Worked example

Figure In the example
The zone’s groundwater ceiling 12,000 acre-feet
Use areas in the zone 600
The account’s use areas in the zone 30
The account’s groundwater allocation 12,000 × 30 / 600 = 600 acre-feet

What is charged against an allocation

{% if 'wells' in enabled_modules %}{% endif %} {% if 'wells' in enabled_modules %}{% endif %}
Topic How it works
Groundwater extraction OpenH2O charges a groundwater allocation with groundwater extraction: the meter reading where a well has a meter, and OpenH2O’s estimate where it has none.
Surface water deliveries OpenH2O counts surface water deliveries only against a surface water ceiling.
Remaining allocation An account’s remaining allocation is its allocation minus the groundwater extraction on its use areas. A negative figure means the account has extracted more than its allocation.
Carry-over OpenH2O adds the carry-over from the previous water year to a zone’s remaining groundwater allocation, but not to an account’s.
A zone with no ceiling When a zone has no groundwater ceiling for the water year, OpenH2O shows a dash for its accounts’ allocations.
