How ceilings are set and divided

Topic How it works
Ceilings A district sets an allocation ceiling on {% if 'accounting' in enabled_modules %}Allocations{% else %}Allocations{% endif %} for each zone, water type and water year.
An account’s share OpenH2O calculates an account’s groundwater allocation in a zone by multiplying the zone’s groundwater ceiling by the number of the account’s use areas in the zone and dividing by the number of all use areas in the zone.
More than one zone An account with use areas in more than one zone receives the sum of its allocations from each zone.
Surface water OpenH2O does not divide a surface water ceiling among accounts. The zone’s page compares that ceiling with the deliveries recorded on the zone’s use areas for the water year.

Worked example

Figure In the example
The zone’s groundwater ceiling 12,000 acre-feet
Use areas in the zone 600
The account’s use areas in the zone 30
The account’s groundwater allocation 12,000 × 30 / 600 = 600 acre-feet

What an allocation is charged with

{% if 'wells' in enabled_modules %}{% endif %}
Topic How it works
Groundwater extracted OpenH2O charges a groundwater allocation with groundwater extracted: the meter reading where a well is metered, and the figure OpenH2O calculates where a well has no meter.
Canal deliveries OpenH2O never charges canal deliveries against a groundwater allocation.
Remaining allocation An account’s remaining allocation is its allocation minus the groundwater extracted on its use areas. A negative remaining allocation means the account has extracted more than its allocation.
Carry-over A zone’s remaining groundwater allocation includes the carry-over from the previous water year, but an account’s remaining allocation does not.
No ceiling Where a zone has no groundwater ceiling for the water year, OpenH2O shows its accounts’ allocations as a dash, not as zero.
