How OpenH2O calculates a month

Step How OpenH2O does it
Crop ET OpenH2O reads each field’s crop ET for the month from OpenET.
ETAW OpenH2O subtracts effective precipitation from crop ET. The method for effective precipitation is set on Methodology Settings, and the default is the USDA-SCS method.
ETAW met by surface water OpenH2O multiplies the month’s surface water delivery by the field’s irrigation efficiency. The result, up to the month’s ETAW, is ETAW met by surface water. The part of the delivery lost to irrigation efficiency is deep percolation.
ETAW met by groundwater On a field with a well, the ETAW that surface water does not meet is ETAW met by groundwater. On a field with no well, the dashboard counts it as water use with no supply reported.
Groundwater extraction Where the well has a meter, the meter reading is the extraction. Otherwise OpenH2O divides ETAW met by groundwater by the field’s irrigation efficiency, and the difference is deep percolation.
A field with no crop OpenH2O treats a field with no crop on file as having no irrigated water use and sets its figures for the month to zero.
The surplus When effective precipitation and ETAW met by surface water together exceed crop ET, OpenH2O records the surplus in two parts: effective precipitation in excess of crop ET, and surface water in excess of ETAW. {% if show_settings %}Settings explained{% else %}Settings explained{% endif %} describes the setting that decides what happens to surface water in excess of ETAW.

How a headgate total is divided

Step How OpenH2O does it
Canal losses OpenH2O first takes the canal’s evaporation, seepage and spill off the total taken at the headgate. Evaporation and spill are written off, and seepage goes to the zone’s seepage pool.
Default losses Until a canal’s own losses are entered on its point of diversion, seepage is 25 percent for an unlined canal, 5 percent for a lined canal and 0 for a pipe. Evaporation is 1 percent for an open canal and 0 for a pipe.
Fields’ own records Next, OpenH2O subtracts any delivery recorded for a field itself, and divides only the remainder among the other fields.
The division OpenH2O divides the remainder among the other fields in proportion to each field’s ETAW. No field receives more than its ETAW divided by its irrigation efficiency.
Water left over Water left after every field has received its ETAW divided by its irrigation efficiency is shown on the point of diversion’s page, and no field is charged with it.
A month with no ETAW In a month when no field on the headgate has any ETAW, OpenH2O divides the remainder by the shares entered on the point of diversion, or evenly when no shares are entered.

Diversions with no meter

Case How OpenH2O does it
The estimate When a diversion has no meter and no volume recorded for a month, OpenH2O estimates the month’s delivery as the water its fields needed, plus the canal’s losses. A field’s need is its ETAW divided by its irrigation efficiency, or its own recorded delivery where it has one.
The water right’s limits Where the diversion’s water right has a face value, the estimate stops at the face value left for the water year. A month wholly outside the right’s direct diversion season is estimated at zero.
No estimate OpenH2O estimates no delivery at a diversion whose water right is inactive or revoked. A diversion with no water right is estimated without a limit.
A recorded volume A volume entered for the month, including zero, takes the place of the estimate.
