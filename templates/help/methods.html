How OpenH2O calculates a month

Topic How it works
Crop ET OpenH2O reads each field’s crop ET for the month from OpenET.
ETAW ETAW is crop ET minus effective precipitation. By default, effective precipitation is estimated with the USDA-SCS method.
ETAW met by surface water The field’s surface water delivery is its share of a headgate total, divided as the next table describes. OpenH2O multiplies the delivery by the field’s irrigation efficiency, and ETAW met by surface water is that product or the month’s ETAW, whichever is smaller.
Deep percolation from a delivery The share of the delivery that the crop cannot use at the field’s irrigation efficiency is deep percolation.
ETAW met by groundwater On a field with a well, the ETAW that surface water does not meet is ETAW met by groundwater. On a field with no well, that ETAW appears on the dashboard as water use with no supply reported.
Groundwater extraction Where the well has a meter, the meter reading is the extraction. Otherwise OpenH2O divides ETAW met by groundwater by the field’s irrigation efficiency to get the extraction, and the extraction minus ETAW met by groundwater is deep percolation.
A field with no crop When a field has no crop on file, OpenH2O sets the month's remainder to zero. The field's crop ET and rain are still recorded.
The surplus When effective precipitation plus the delivery times irrigation efficiency exceeds crop ET, OpenH2O records the surplus in two parts: effective precipitation in excess of crop ET, and surface water in excess of ETAW. What happens to surface water in excess of ETAW depends on a setting described in {% if show_settings %}Settings explained{% else %}Settings explained{% endif %}.

How a headgate total is divided

Topic How it works
Canal losses OpenH2O first subtracts the canal’s evaporation, seepage and spill from the headgate total. Evaporation and spill are charged to no one, and seepage goes to the zone’s seepage pool.
Default losses If no losses are entered on the point of diversion, OpenH2O uses 25 percent seepage for an unlined canal, 5 percent for a lined canal and none for a pipe. It uses 1 percent evaporation for an open canal, none for a pipe, and no spill.
Fields’ own records Next, OpenH2O subtracts any delivery recorded for an individual field.
The division OpenH2O divides the remainder among the other fields in proportion to each field’s ETAW. No field receives more than its ETAW divided by its irrigation efficiency.
Water left over If water remains after every field has received its ETAW divided by its irrigation efficiency, OpenH2O shows it on the point of diversion’s page and charges it to no field.
A month with no ETAW In a month when no field on the headgate has any ETAW, OpenH2O divides the remainder according to the shares entered on the point of diversion, or evenly when none are entered.

Diversions with no meter

Topic How it works
A recorded volume When a volume is entered for the month, even zero, OpenH2O uses it and does not estimate the delivery.
The estimate When a diversion has no meter and no volume recorded for a month, OpenH2O estimates the delivery as the water its fields need plus the canal’s losses. A field’s need is its ETAW divided by its irrigation efficiency, or its own recorded delivery where it has one.
The water right’s limits Where the diversion’s water right has a face value, the estimate cannot exceed the face value remaining for the water year. A month wholly outside the right’s direct diversion season is estimated at zero. When a diversion has no water right on file, its estimate has no limit.
No estimate OpenH2O does not estimate a delivery at a diversion whose water right is inactive or revoked.
