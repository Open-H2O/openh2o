Step by step

Every field goes through the same steps each month. Steps 1 to 4 make up the calculation plan, the list shown on Methodology Settings. Step 5 runs after the plan.

1
Crop water use for the month.

The satellite estimate of what the crop used this month, in acre-feet. Every supply, whatever its source, is matched against it.

2
Take off the rain the crop could use.

Rain for the area is estimated from weather data, not read from a gauge on the field. The share the crop could use depends on the method chosen on Methodology Settings: all of the month's rain, a fixed fraction of it, or the USDA-SCS soil-storage method, which is the default. What remains after this step is crop water use after rain. The calculation records it as net consumptive use and uses it to weight each field's share of a headgate's total.

3
Take off the canal water the crop could use.

The delivery on record for the month, multiplied by the field's irrigation efficiency. Not all the canal water reaches the crop: some soaks past the roots and some runs off the end of the field. The efficiency comes from the field's irrigation method where one is on file. The method table is published and has eighteen entries, from 60% for conventional furrow to 95% for subsurface drip. A field with no method on file uses the deployment's default on Delivery Settings, 75% unless changed. The month's calculation records the delivery, the efficiency it applied and the result, so the figure can be read back later even after the setting changes.

A switch on this step in the calculation plan turns the efficiency on or off. With the switch on, only the canal water the crop could use is subtracted. With it off, the whole delivery is subtracted and the month's calculation records no separate delivered figure. A plan seeded on a new deployment has the switch on. It is not yet shown on the Methodology Settings screen and is set in the plan directly.

4
What is left, by what the field has.

Two plan steps, shown as two entries on Methodology Settings. First, a field with no crop on record is set to zero. Then the remainder is floored at zero. Where rain and canal water together exceeded what the crop used, the remainder is zero. The month's calculation records the excess in two parts: rain the crop did not need, kept for information, and canal water beyond what the crop could use, which the next section covers. What a positive remainder becomes depends on the field. On a field with a well and no meter reading for the month, it is groundwater consumed. On a field with no well, it is water use recorded, no supply reported. On a month with a meter reading on record, it is kept for comparison only: the reading stands and no groundwater row is written.

5
From consumed to extracted.

This step runs after the plan, as part of the calculation itself. Its setting is on Delivery Settings. On a field with a well and no meter reading for the month, groundwater consumed is divided by the share of pumped groundwater the crop consumes, 80% unless changed. The result is groundwater extracted. A pump has to lift more water than the crop ends up using, because some water soaks past the roots. The difference between the two figures is recorded on the month's calculation as deep percolation. The groundwater allocation is charged the groundwater extracted that was stamped when the month was calculated. A change to the setting takes effect the next time a month is calculated. A month already calculated keeps its stamped figure until it is re-run.

The plan is editable. Steps 1 to 4 can be reordered, switched on or off and tuned on the {% if 'accounting' in enabled_modules %}Methodology Settings{% else %}Methodology Settings{% endif %} page. The rain method and the floor have settings there; the other steps have none on that screen. Every month calculated after a change carries the plan it ran under.

Canal water beyond what the crop could use

When rain and the canal water the crop could use add up to more than the crop used, the canal part of the excess is recorded on the month's calculation as canal water beyond what the crop could use. What happens to that amount is one setting on Delivery Settings, with three values. Each month's calculation records the value it ran under. A change takes effect the next time a month is calculated, and a month already calculated keeps what it recorded until it is re-run.

  • Not credited. The default, and the demonstration's setting. The amount is recorded on the month's calculation and nothing further happens: no credit to anyone, and no charge.
  • Credited to the landowner, less a share left in the basin. The share left in the basin is 10% unless changed. A field with a well receives the credited amount on its own ledger. A field with no well cannot pump a credit back, so its credited amount goes to the shared account of its management-area zone. A field with no such zone is credited nothing.
  • Its own line. The amount appears on the field's page as a line of its own, neither credited nor charged.

The field's water balance is the same under all three values. The setting changes only what the amount is called and whether anyone is credited.

Three endings

A month with a meter reading

The reading is the month's groundwater record, and the allocation is charged the reading as recorded. The calculation still runs and is kept on the month's page for comparison. It writes no groundwater row and stamps no extracted figure, so nothing is counted twice.

A well, no meter reading

The remainder goes on the field's ledger as groundwater consumed, with a note saying so. Groundwater extracted is stamped on the month's calculation beside it, and that is the figure the allocation is charged. A month in which rain and canal water covered the crop writes a line of 0.00 acre-feet and states that no groundwater extraction was derived.

No well

The remainder is written as water use recorded, no supply reported. It is stored on the month's calculation and shown on the field's page. The label means only that the supplies on record did not account for crop water use; it says nothing about why. It is never written as pumping, and it is never a finding about the grower.

Sharing one canal total across many fields

A district records what left each headgate, not what reached each field. Each month the software splits each headgate's total among the fields it serves. It first takes off the canal's losses, using the evaporation, seepage and spill shares on file for that point of diversion. Seepage goes to the seepage pool of the management-area zone that holds the canal and counts toward that zone's available water; evaporation and spill are credited to no one. A field with its own delivery record for the month keeps that record. The rest is divided among the other fields the headgate serves. If it is less than those fields could use, each field's share is in proportion to its crop water use after rain. If it covers them all, each field receives what it could use (its crop water use after rain divided by its irrigation efficiency), and what is left over is recorded against the headgate. If no field on the headgate has an estimated use on record for the month, as is also the case before the calculation first runs, each field instead gets the fixed share on file for it. Each field's ledger row names the rule that produced its share.

The total is honored as recorded

The headgate's total is never altered. The split decides only how much of it each field is charged with. Anything left after every field has what it could use is recorded against the headgate rather than dropped. Each canal's page shows every month's total in its parts: the canal's losses, the fields' own records, what was divided up among the other fields, and what was beyond what the crops could use.

A diversion with no meter

Where a point of diversion has no measuring device in service and no volume entered for a month, the software estimates that month's delivery from the fields it serves. For each field it divides crop water use after rain by the field's irrigation efficiency, adds the results, and increases the sum to allow for the canal's losses. The estimate is capped at what remains of the water right's face value for the water year. Where the right has a direct season on record, the estimate is zero for any month outside it. Use above the cap counts as groundwater on a field with a well; on a field without one, it counts as water use recorded, no supply reported. The estimate is saved as a provisional record labeled as an estimate. A volume entered for that month replaces it. No estimate is made under a curtailed water right or where no water right is linked.

What this platform does not do yet

  • A flag when an estimate reaches the face value. An estimated delivery that reaches the water right's face value is capped today. A flag on the water right's page, for the district to review, is not yet built.
  • A flat yearly rain allowance. Rain is taken off month by month, as described in step 2. A single yearly allowance is not yet built.

This platform does not submit reports to the state.
