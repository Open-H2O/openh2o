The settings behind each figure

Topic How it works
Calculation steps OpenH2O calculates each month's figures by following the steps listed on {% if 'accounting' in enabled_modules %}Methodology Settings{% else %}Methodology Settings{% endif %}. A district can reorder those steps or switch any of them off.
Effective precipitation {% if 'accounting' in enabled_modules %}Methodology Settings{% else %}Methodology Settings{% endif %} also sets the method for estimating effective precipitation: all of the month's rain, a fixed percentage of it, or the USDA-SCS soil-storage method, which is the default.
Irrigation efficiency Each field's irrigation efficiency comes from its irrigation method on file. A field with no irrigation method on file uses the default efficiency on {% if 'accounting' in enabled_modules %}Delivery Settings{% else %}Delivery Settings{% endif %}, 75 percent unless the district changes it.{% if 'surface' in enabled_modules %} OpenH2O uses the same irrigation efficiency for a field's surface water and its groundwater.{% endif %}
Delivery lost to efficiency OpenH2O records the share of a surface water delivery lost to irrigation efficiency as deep percolation and does not charge or credit any account for it.
Groundwater extraction Where a well has no meter reading, OpenH2O estimates groundwater extraction by dividing ETAW met by groundwater by the field's irrigation efficiency. OpenH2O records the difference as deep percolation.
Past months Each calculated month stores the settings it was calculated with. A changed setting affects a past month only when that month is recalculated.

When effective precipitation and surface water exceed crop ET

Topic How it works
The remainder When effective precipitation and the delivery times irrigation efficiency together exceed crop ET, OpenH2O sets the remainder to zero and records the surplus in two parts: effective precipitation in excess of crop ET, and surface water in excess of ETAW.
Surface water in excess of ETAW A setting on {% if 'accounting' in enabled_modules %}Delivery Settings{% else %}Delivery Settings{% endif %} determines how OpenH2O treats surface water in excess of ETAW. By default, OpenH2O credits that water to no one. A district can instead credit it to the landowner, less a share left in the basin (10 percent by default), or show it as a separate line on the field's page without crediting it.
Where a credit goes When surface water in excess of ETAW is credited, a field with a well receives the credit on its own ledger. A field without a well has the credit added to its zone's shared account.

How a headgate total is divided

Topic How it works
Canal losses Before dividing a headgate total among the fields it serves, OpenH2O deducts the canal's losses. Evaporation and spill are written off, and seepage goes to the zone's seepage pool.
Fixed shares In a month when no field on the headgate has any ETAW, OpenH2O divides the total by each field's fixed share on file.

Diversions with no meter

Topic How it works
The estimate For an unmetered diversion, OpenH2O estimates the month's delivery from the ETAW of the fields the diversion serves, adds the canal's losses, and caps the estimate at the water right's remaining face value for the water year.
When there is no estimate OpenH2O estimates a delivery only when the diversion is linked to an active or curtailed water right, and never for a month covered by a curtailment order.
