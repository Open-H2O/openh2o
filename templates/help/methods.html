How OpenH2O calculates a month

Topic How it works
Crop ET OpenH2O uses the crop ET from OpenET recorded for each use area and month. The next table says how a month’s figure is recorded and how a newer one is accepted.
ETAW ETAW is crop ET minus effective precipitation. By default, effective precipitation is estimated with the USDA-SCS method.
The effective precipitation formula The USDA-SCS method works in millimeters. With P the month’s precipitation, ET the month’s recorded OpenET crop ET (the satellite figure, even in a fallow month) and D the soil storage in inches (3.0 unless the setting is changed), OpenH2O first computes a storage factor, SF = 0.531747 + 0.295164 × D − 0.057697 × D² + 0.003804 × D³. Effective precipitation is SF × (1.25 × P0.824 − 2.93) × 10(0.000955 × ET), with P0.824 taken as 0 when P is 0. The result is held at or below the smaller of P and ET and at or above 0, then converted to acre-feet over the use area’s acres. The formula is evaluated in ordinary binary floating point.
ETAW met by surface water The use area’s surface water delivery is its share of a headgate total, divided as the next table describes. OpenH2O multiplies the delivery by the use area’s irrigation efficiency, and ETAW met by surface water is that product or the month’s ETAW, whichever is smaller.
ETAW met by groundwater On a use area with a well, the ETAW that surface water does not meet is ETAW met by groundwater. On a use area with no well, that ETAW appears on the dashboard as water use with no supply reported.
Groundwater extraction Where the well has a meter, the use area’s share of the well’s meter reading is the extraction, divided evenly among its use areas where no shares are entered. Otherwise OpenH2O divides ETAW met by groundwater by the use area’s irrigation efficiency to calculate the extraction. The extraction minus ETAW met by groundwater is the pumped water not consumed, which the dashboard and the account pages list under Uses.
A fallow period OpenH2O counts a use area’s crop ET as 0 in each month of a fallow period, from its start month through its end month. A period with no end month lasts until someone ends it on the use area’s page. The satellite figure is still recorded with the month’s calculation.
The surplus When effective precipitation plus the delivery times irrigation efficiency exceeds crop ET, OpenH2O records the surplus in two parts: effective precipitation in excess of crop ET, and surface water in excess of ETAW. What happens to surface water in excess of ETAW depends on a setting described in {% if show_settings %}Settings explained{% else %}Settings explained{% endif %}.
Rounding OpenH2O stores acre-feet to four decimal places and rounds half to even: a figure exactly halfway between two values goes to the one whose last digit is even, so 2.50285 is stored as 2.5028. Pages that print two decimal places round half up, which changes only what is printed. On a use area’s page, the By month table rounds each column so its months add to the water year’s figure: each month is rounded down, and the months closest to rounding up take the remaining hundredths.
The order of rounding on a run Gross crop ET and effective precipitation are carried unrounded through the calculation. The canal water counted is rounded once, as the delivery times the use area’s irrigation efficiency. When canal water was counted and the running figure after that step is less than 0.0001 acre-feet from 0 on either side, and is not exactly 0, OpenH2O sets it to 0. The figures stored for the month are then each rounded on their own: gross ET, effective precipitation, ETAW (the stored gross ET minus the stored effective precipitation), the final figure after the floor, the groundwater extraction (the stored final figure divided by irrigation efficiency), the pumped water not consumed (the extraction minus the stored final figure), surface water in excess of ETAW, and the unmet demand. The surface water stored for the month is the delivery times irrigation efficiency in full, even where it exceeds ETAW.
A month with no delivery When a month has no canal water, its delivery is stored as 0.0000 and its irrigation efficiency is left blank.

How OpenH2O keeps OpenET’s figure for a month

Topic How it works
Recorded once OpenH2O records the first crop ET figure OpenET sends for each use area and month. It also records the day the figure was received. The month’s calculation and its ET estimate entry use that recorded figure and no other.
Why OpenET revises a month OpenET may revise a month’s figure for about 120 days after the month ends. It may also release a new version that changes older months. It releases at most one new version a year.
Provisional and Final A recorded figure is Provisional when OpenET sent it within 120 days of the month’s end. It is Final when OpenET sent it later. OpenH2O leaves a figure unmarked if the day it was received is unknown. A Provisional or unmarked figure becomes Final when OpenET sends the same figure after the 120 days.
A newer figure Asking OpenET for a month again does not change the recorded figure. The month’s calculation page shows any different figure OpenET sends beside the recorded one. OpenH2O treats figures that match to the hundredth of an acre-foot as the same. The OpenET sync command lists every month with a different figure.
Accepting a newer figure A person who can correct records may accept the newer figure on the month’s calculation page. Acceptance takes a reason of 10 to 500 characters. OpenH2O replaces the recorded figure and the month’s ET estimate entry as corrections. The page keeps the earlier figure struck through with the day, the person and the reason. OpenH2O recalculates the month at once.
A finalized water year In a finalized water year the newer figure is shown and nothing changes. Only an administrator can accept it. The acceptance needs a reason and is recorded as an exception to the closed year.
ET estimate entries A person cannot type an ET figure. The hand entry form, the ledger import and the administration site cannot create or change one. An entry written before this version can differ from the recorded figure. The ledger and the month’s calculation page then show both. A person who can correct records may correct the entry with a reason.
The upgrade to this version The upgrade recorded each month’s figure from the crop ET its last calculation used. It recorded the newest figure OpenET had sent for any month never calculated. No figure changed. These months read “date received not recorded” on the calculation page.
Rainfall OpenH2O does not record gridMET rainfall this way. A new gridMET figure for a month changes that month’s next calculation.

How a headgate total is divided

Topic How it works
Canal losses OpenH2O first subtracts the canal’s evaporation, seepage and spill from the water kept at the headgate, which is the water taken less the water returned. Evaporation and spill are not charged to anyone, and seepage goes to the zone’s seepage pool.
Default losses If no losses are entered on the point of diversion, OpenH2O uses 25 percent seepage for an unlined canal, 5 percent for a lined canal and none for a pipe or a canal with no lining stated. It uses 1 percent evaporation for an open canal, none for a pipe, and no spill.
Use areas’ own records Next, OpenH2O subtracts any delivery recorded for an individual use area.
The division By default, OpenH2O divides the remainder among the other use areas by the share entered for each use area, or by acreage when no use area’s share has been changed from 1.0. When the point of diversion is set to divide by crop ET, OpenH2O divides the remainder in proportion to each use area’s ETAW, and no use area receives more than its ETAW divided by its irrigation efficiency.
The shares entered OpenH2O divides by the shares entered when any use area’s entered share differs from the default of 1.0. When none does, it divides by acreage, or evenly among the use areas when none has an acreage on record. Each use area’s weight is its share, or its acreage, divided by the total of them, rounded to four decimal places. When OpenH2O divides by acreage, a use area without an acreage on record is given 0 acre-feet. The rounding difference in the weights goes to the use area whose ID sorts last as text. Each use area’s amount is the water times its rounded weight, rounded to four decimal places, and the use area whose ID sorts last takes what is left, so the amounts add to the total exactly.
A use area marked fallow In a month a use area is marked fallow, OpenH2O leaves it out of a division by shares and divides the water among the other use areas. When every use area without its own delivery record is fallow that month, OpenH2O shows the water on the point of diversion’s page and does not charge it to a use area.
Dividing by crop ET, in detail Each use area’s ceiling is its ETAW divided by its irrigation efficiency, rounded to four decimal places. When the water covers every ceiling, each use area receives its ceiling. When it does not, OpenH2O divides all of it in proportion to ETAW, holds any use area whose share would pass its ceiling at that ceiling, and divides what is left among the other use areas in proportion to ETAW, repeating until every use area is at or under its ceiling. Each share is then rounded to four decimal places and held at its ceiling. The rounding difference goes to the use area with the most room below its ceiling, to the use area whose APN sorts last as text when two have equal room, and on to the next use area when the first cannot take all of it.
Water left over When the point of diversion divides by crop ET and water remains after every use area has received its ETAW divided by its irrigation efficiency, OpenH2O shows it on the point of diversion’s page and does not charge it to a use area.
A point that does not serve a use area When a point of diversion does not serve a use area, OpenH2O subtracts the canal’s losses and does not divide the rest. It shows the rest on the point of diversion’s page as delivered to the named site the point feeds, or as Not delivered to a use area, and does not credit it to anyone.
A month with no ETAW In a month when no use area on the headgate has any ETAW, OpenH2O divides the remainder according to the shares entered on the point of diversion, or by acreage when none are entered.
A month recorded as 0 When a diversion’s volume for a month is 0, OpenH2O does not record a delivery to any use area for that month.
The date of a divided delivery A divided delivery carries the date of the diversion record it was divided from, whatever day in the month that is, and that record’s water year.

Diversions with no meter

Topic How it works
A recorded volume When a volume is entered for the month, even zero, OpenH2O uses it.
No volume When a diversion has no meter reading and no volume entered for a month, OpenH2O does not record a delivery for it, and the use areas it serves do not receive canal water from it that month. OpenH2O does not estimate a delivery from the crop need of the use areas.
An earlier estimate An earlier version of OpenH2O estimated a delivery for such a month. Upgrading deletes those records from every water year that is not finalized, and the next calculation of that year removes the canal water divided from them. A finalized water year keeps them, marked as estimated, and its months keep the figures they were calculated with, in which the estimate was not subtracted from ETAW.
