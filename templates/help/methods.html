Step by step

Every field runs through the same steps each month. Steps 1 to 4 are the calculation plan, the list an operator sees on Methodology Settings. Step 5 runs after the plan.

1
Crop water use for the month.

The satellite estimate for this field, in acre-feet. It is the demand every supply is matched against, whatever the source, and it is an estimate, never a reading.

2
Take off the rain the crop could use.

Rain for the area comes from weather data, not a gauge on the field. The share of it the crop could use is worked out by the method set on Methodology Settings: all of the month's rain, a flat fraction of it, or the USDA-SCS soil-storage method, which is the default. What is left after this step is crop water use after rain, which the platform records as net consumptive use. It is also the figure the split of each headgate's total reads.

3
Take off the canal water the crop could use.

The delivery on record for the month, times the field's irrigation efficiency. Not all the canal water reaches the crop: some soaks past the roots and some runs off the end of the field. The efficiency is the field's own where one is set (the field's irrigation method, from a published table of eighteen methods, 60% for conventional furrow up to 95% for subsurface drip); otherwise the deployment's default on Delivery Settings, 75% unless changed. The month's calculation records the delivery, the efficiency it used and the result, so the figure can be read back later whatever the setting says by then.

The switch: whether this step applies the efficiency at all is a setting on the step itself in the calculation plan. On, only the canal water the crop could use comes off; off, the whole delivery comes off and the month's calculation records no separate delivered figure. A plan seeded on a new deployment has it on. The switch is not yet on the Methodology Settings screen; it is set in the plan directly.

4
What is left, by what the field has.

Two plan steps, shown as two entries on Methodology Settings: a field with no crop on record is set to zero, and then the remainder is floored at zero. Where rain and canal water together came to more than the crop used, the remainder is zero and the excess is recorded on the month's calculation in two parts: rain the crop did not need, kept as information, and canal water beyond what the crop could use, which the next section covers. What a positive remainder becomes depends on the field: groundwater consumed on a field with a well and no meter reading for the month; water use recorded, no supply reported on a field with no well; and on a month with a meter reading on record, a figure kept for comparison only, because the reading stands and no groundwater row is written.

5
From consumed to extracted.

This step runs after the plan, in the calculation itself, and its value is on Delivery Settings, not Methodology Settings. On a field with a well and no meter reading for the month, groundwater consumed is divided by the share of pumped groundwater the crop consumes (80% unless changed). The result is groundwater extracted. A pump has to lift more water than the crop ends up using, because some water soaks past the roots. The difference between the two figures is recorded on the month's calculation as deep percolation. The groundwater allocation is charged groundwater extracted, at the figure stamped when the month was calculated. A change to the setting applies when a month is next calculated; a month already calculated keeps its stamped figure until it is re-run.

The plan is not hard-coded. An operator reorders, switches and tunes steps 1 to 4 on the {% if 'accounting' in enabled_modules %}Methodology Settings{% else %}Methodology Settings{% endif %} page (the rain method and the floor are editable there; the other steps have no knobs on that screen), and every month calculated after a change carries the plan it ran under.

Canal water beyond what the crop could use

When rain and the canal water the crop could use add up to more than the crop used, the canal part of the excess is recorded on the month's calculation as canal water beyond what the crop could use. What happens to it is one setting on Delivery Settings. Every month's calculation records the value it ran under. A change applies when a month is next calculated; a month already calculated keeps what it recorded until it is re-run.

  • Not credited. The default, and the demonstration's setting. The amount is recorded on the month's calculation and nothing else happens. It is nobody's credit and it is not charged.
  • Credited to the landowner, less a share left in the basin. The share left in the basin is 10% unless changed. A field with a well gets the credited amount on its own ledger. A field with no well has no well to pump it back, so its credited amount goes to its management-area zone's shared account instead; a field with no such zone has nowhere to credit it, and nothing is credited.
  • Its own line. The amount is shown on the field's page as its own line. It is not a credit and is not charged.

Under every value the field's water balance reads the same; the setting changes what the amount is called and where a credit lands, not the balance.

Three endings

A month with a meter reading

The reading is the month's groundwater record and the allocation is charged it as recorded. The calculation still runs and is kept on the month's page for comparison, but it writes no groundwater row and stamps no extracted figure, so nothing is double counted.

A well, no meter reading

The remainder is written to the field's ledger as groundwater consumed, with a sentence saying so. Groundwater extracted is stamped on the month's calculation beside it and is what the allocation is charged. A month where rain and canal water covered the crop writes a 0.00 acre-feet line and says that no groundwater extraction was derived.

No well

Water use recorded, no supply reported. The remainder is stored on the month's calculation and shown on the field's page. It says the arithmetic did not close from the supplies on record and nothing about why. It is never written as pumping and it is never a finding about the grower.

Sharing one canal total across many fields

A district records what left each headgate, not what reached each field. The software first takes the canal's losses off each month's headgate total, using the shares for evaporation, seepage and spill on file for that point of diversion. Seepage is added to the district's seepage pool for the zone that holds the canal, and counts toward what that zone has available; evaporation and spill are credited to no one. A field with a delivery record of its own for the month keeps it, and what is left is divided among the other fields the headgate serves. When a headgate's canal total is less than its fields could use, it is divided up among them in proportion to each field's crop water use after rain. When it covers every field, each field gets what it could use (its crop water use after rain divided by its irrigation efficiency) and the rest is recorded against the headgate. Where no field the headgate serves has an estimated use on record for the month, which is also the case before the calculation first runs, the total is divided by the fixed share on file for each field instead. The ledger row for each field's share says which of the two it was.

The total is honored as recorded

The headgate's total is never changed. The split only decides how much of it each field is charged with. Anything left after every field has what it could use is recorded against the headgate, not dropped. Each canal's page shows every month's total in its parts: the canal's losses, the fields' own records, what was divided up, and what was beyond what the crops could use.

A diversion with no meter

Where a point of diversion has no measuring device in service and no volume entered for a month, the software estimates the month's delivery from the crop water use of the fields it serves: each field's use after rain divided by its irrigation efficiency, grossed up for the canal's losses. The estimate is capped at what remains of the water right's face value for the water year, and is zero outside the right's direct season when one is recorded. Use above the cap is counted as groundwater where a field has a well, otherwise as water use recorded, no supply reported. The estimate is saved as a provisional record labeled as an estimate, and a volume entered for that month always replaces it. No estimate is made under a water right that is curtailed, or where no water right is linked.

What this platform does not do yet

  • A flag when an estimate reaches the face value. An estimated diversion that reaches its water right's face value is capped today; a flag on the water right's page, for the district to look at, is next.
  • A flat yearly rain allowance. Later. Rain is taken off month by month as described in step 2; a single yearly allowance is not built.

This platform does not submit reports to the state.
