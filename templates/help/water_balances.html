How a month is calculated

Step What OpenH2O does
1 For each field and month, OpenH2O starts with OpenET's satellite estimate of the crop's water use.
2 It subtracts the rain the crop could use, which is estimated from weather data.
3 It subtracts the canal water the crop could use: the field's delivery multiplied by its irrigation efficiency.
4 What is left after those subtractions is the remainder.

What the remainder becomes

The field has What happens
A well and no meter reading The remainder is groundwater consumed. Groundwater consumed divided by the share of pumped water the crop consumes gives groundwater extracted, which is charged against the groundwater allocation.
A meter reading OpenH2O uses the reading and keeps its own calculation beside it for comparison.
No well The remainder is shown as "water use recorded, no supply reported" and is never recorded as pumping.

How canal deliveries are divided

Case What OpenH2O does
A headgate total A district records each headgate's delivery as one monthly total, and OpenH2O divides that total among the fields the headgate serves in proportion to each field's crop water use after rain.
A field's own record A field with its own delivery record keeps that record instead.
A diversion with no meter When a diversion has no meter and no volume entered for the month, OpenH2O estimates the delivery from the fields it serves, up to what is left of the water right's face value.

Every figure that comes from the satellite estimate is labeled as an estimate.
