How a month is calculated

Step What OpenH2O does
1 For each field and month, OpenH2O starts with OpenET's satellite estimate of crop ET, or 0 for a field marked fallow.
2 OpenH2O subtracts effective precipitation, estimated from gridMET rainfall. The result is ETAW.
3 OpenH2O assigns ETAW first to surface water, up to the field's delivery times its irrigation efficiency. A delivery OpenH2O estimated for a diversion with no meter is not counted in this step.
4 The ETAW that surface water does not meet is the remainder.

What the remainder becomes

The field has What happens
A well and no meter reading The remainder is ETAW met by groundwater. OpenH2O divides ETAW met by groundwater by the field's irrigation efficiency to estimate groundwater extraction, and charges that extraction against the groundwater allocation.
A meter reading OpenH2O uses the reading and keeps its own calculation beside it for comparison.
No well The remainder is shown as "water use recorded, no supply reported" and is never recorded as pumping.

How surface water deliveries are divided

Case What OpenH2O does
A headgate total OpenH2O keeps each headgate's delivery as one monthly total. It subtracts the canal's losses and any delivery recorded for an individual field, then divides the remainder among the other fields by the share entered for each field, or by acreage where none is entered. A headgate set to divide by crop ET divides it in proportion to each field's ETAW, up to what each field can use.
A field's own record A field with its own delivery record keeps that record instead.
A diversion with no meter When a diversion has no meter and no volume entered for the month, OpenH2O estimates the delivery from the fields it serves. If the diversion's water right has a face value, OpenH2O caps the estimate at the face value remaining for the water year.

Every figure that comes from the satellite estimate is labeled as an estimate.
