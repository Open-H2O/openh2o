How a month is calculated

Step What OpenH2O does
1 For each use area and month, OpenH2O starts with OpenET's satellite estimate of crop ET, or 0 in a month the use area is marked fallow.
2 OpenH2O subtracts effective precipitation, estimated from gridMET rainfall. The result is ETAW.
3 OpenH2O assigns ETAW first to surface water, up to the use area's delivery times its irrigation efficiency.
4 The ETAW that surface water does not meet is the remainder.

What the remainder becomes

The use area has What happens
A well and no meter reading The remainder is ETAW met by groundwater. OpenH2O divides ETAW met by groundwater by the use area's irrigation efficiency to estimate groundwater extraction, and charges that extraction against the groundwater allocation. The dashboard counts the extraction as the use area's groundwater supply, and counts the extraction minus ETAW met by groundwater as pumped water not consumed.
A meter reading OpenH2O uses the reading and keeps its own calculation beside it for comparison. The dashboard counts the reading as the use area's groundwater supply, and counts the part of the reading beyond the ETAW that surface water did not meet as pumped water not consumed.
No well The remainder is shown as "water use recorded, no supply reported" and is never recorded as pumping.

A use area with a well and no meter reading balances to zero when it did not receive surface water. When it did receive surface water, its balance is the part of the delivery that OpenH2O does not count toward ETAW. A negative balance means the uses were more than the supplies on record.

How surface water deliveries are divided

Case What OpenH2O does
A headgate total OpenH2O records each headgate’s delivery as one monthly total: the water taken less any water returned to the stream. It subtracts the canal’s losses and any delivery recorded for an individual use area, then divides the remainder among the other use areas by the share entered for each use area, or by acreage where none is entered. A use area marked fallow that month does not receive a share. When a point of diversion does not serve a use area, the remainder is not divided. OpenH2O shows it on the point of diversion’s page as delivered to the named site the point feeds, or as Not delivered to a use area, and does not credit it to anyone. A headgate set to divide by crop ET divides the remainder in proportion to each use area’s ETAW, up to what each use area can use.
A use area's own record A use area with its own delivery record keeps that record instead.
A diversion with no meter When a diversion has no meter reading and no volume entered for the month, OpenH2O does not record a delivery for it, and the use areas it serves do not receive canal water from it that month.

Metered is a meter reading, or water from a diversion record whose method is a measuring device or methodology. Estimated is groundwater OpenH2O calculated from crop ET, or water from a record whose method is an estimate. Unknown is everything else, such as a delivery entered on the Use Ledger, or a record whose method is not stated.

A downloaded ledger file names each calculated row's calculation method and settings in its last column.

Reading the records as of a date

The As of box on account pages, use area pages and the Use Ledger shows the records as they were at the end of a chosen day. Account, zone, well and canal names, settings and map shapes stay as they are today.

A line at the top counts the records changed since that day. Records cannot be edited while a page shows a past date.
