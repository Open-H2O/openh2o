What this site holds

This site shows OpenH2O at work on a real groundwater basin, the Merced Subbasin in the San Joaquin Valley. Its maps, monitoring stations and drinking-water results are public records. Its water accounting is sample data made up for the demonstration, and it names no real water district.

The site is for trying OpenH2O and has no regulatory or legal standing. On the drinking-water screens, a label on each section names its publisher.

Invented sample data

The organizations and people in the water accounting are made up, and so are their wells, water rights, meters and deliveries. Several of these records are placed on real land, rivers and canals.

Record How the demonstration made it
The names of the agencies and districts Each agency and district has a made-up name. The three Groundwater Sustainability Agencies keep their real boundaries.
Owners, addresses and parcel numbers The crop map gives field outlines only. Each field’s owner, address and parcel number are made up.
Crops The demonstration gives each irrigated field a crop from a short list of San Joaquin Valley crops.
Wells The 45 wells and their construction details are made up.
Water rights, points of diversion and deliveries The water rights and deliveries are made up. Each point of diversion is on a real river or canal and carries that waterway’s name.
Water accounts, allocations and the Use Ledger The accounts and allocations are made up. For each field with a well and no meter readings, OpenH2O calculated the groundwater use on the Use Ledger from the field’s crop ET and rainfall.
Crop ET and rainfall, October 2025 to September 2026 The demonstration derived these figures from the year before, with half the rainfall, to show a dry year.
Meters and account contacts The meters and contacts are made up. Each meter’s monthly readings add up to the metered groundwater on the Use Ledger.
Recharge sites and recharge events Each recharge site uses the outline of a real piece of land beside a canal or river. The sites, their events and the volumes recharged are made up.

The drinking-water results

The drinking-water section shows the City of Merced’s laboratory results under the city’s real name, detections included, as the Division of Drinking Water published them. The water accounting on this site belongs to the made-up organizations and says nothing about what the city pumps, owns or owes.

The drinking-water section is not a compliance assessment. OpenH2O keeps each result and each regulatory limit as separate records; it does not compare them and does not publish a verdict. Whether a water system meets a standard is for its regulator to decide.
