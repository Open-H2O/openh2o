The short version

For testing purposes only. This is not any agency's live system. Nothing here is a regulatory filing, a compliance determination, or a legal record.

Some of what you see is genuine public record, carried unchanged from whoever published it. The rest is invented to show how the accounting works — no agency's real ledgers are ours to publish. The two sit side by side, so the tables below say which is which.

The laboratory results are filtered to one water system and to a three-year window, and are otherwise the state's own file, unedited. No result has been added, removed, rounded or adjusted.

Invented sample data

Built to demonstrate the accounting. Every district, agency, farm and well owner in it is invented, so the groundwater side of this demonstration names no real water district at all. The drinking-water side is deliberately the opposite: genuine published laboratory results under the City of Merced's real name. Even there, the accounting figures on this site are not the city's, and are not what it actually pumps, owns or owes.

What Why it is invented
Agricultural wells and their construction details Real private well records are not ours to publish
The names of the agencies and districts Real boundaries, invented agencies — the accounting inside them is invented, so no real agency is named as its author
Water rights, points of diversion and deliveries Placed on real river and canal geometry, but the rights themselves are made up
Parcel owner names and addresses Real shapes, invented owners — the shapes are public, the people are not
Water accounts, allocations and the whole ledger The accounting story the platform exists to show, with no real agency's numbers in it
Meters, calibration dates and account contacts Filler so detail pages read complete
Managed recharge events Real basin shapes, invented volumes

One thing worth being clear about

The drinking-water section names a real utility and shows its real laboratory results, including detections. That is deliberate: a demonstration built on invented chemistry would prove nothing about whether this platform can handle a real state data file.

It is not a compliance assessment. This platform records what a result was and, separately, what a limit is; it does not compare them and does not publish a verdict. Judging a water system against a standard is the regulator's job, and nothing on these screens should be read as that judgement having been made.
