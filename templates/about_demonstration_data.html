What this site holds

This site shows OpenH2O at work on a real groundwater basin, the Merced Subbasin in the San Joaquin Valley. The basin’s boundaries, rivers, canals and fields, its monitoring stations and the drinking-water results are public records. The water accounting is sample data made up for the demonstration, and it names no real water district.

The site is for trying OpenH2O and has no regulatory or legal standing.

Invented sample data

The organizations and people in the water accounting are made up, and so are their accounts, allocations, wells, water rights, meters, deliveries and recharge events.

Record How the demonstration made it
The names of the agencies and districts Each agency and district has a made-up name. The three Groundwater Sustainability Agencies keep their real boundaries.
Owners, addresses and parcel numbers The crop map gives field outlines only, so the demonstration made up each field’s owner, address and parcel number.
Crops The demonstration gave each irrigated field a crop from a short list of San Joaquin Valley crops.
Points of diversion Each point of diversion is on a real river or canal and carries that waterway’s name.
Groundwater use on the Use Ledger Where a field has a well and no meter, the Use Ledger shows the groundwater use that OpenH2O calculated from the field’s crop ET and rainfall.
Crop ET and rainfall, October 2025 to September 2026 The demonstration derived these figures from the year before to show a dry year. Rainfall is cut by half, and winter crop ET is up to 15 percent lower.
Meter readings Each meter’s monthly readings add up to its total on the Use Ledger, so the meter’s page and the ledger agree.
Recharge sites Each recharge site is drawn on the outline of a real plot of land beside a canal or river.

The drinking-water results

The drinking-water screens show the City of Merced’s laboratory results under the city’s real name, including detections, as the Division of Drinking Water published them. A label on each drinking-water section names its publisher. The water accounting on this site belongs to the made-up organizations and says nothing about what the city pumps, owns or owes.

The drinking-water section is not a compliance assessment. OpenH2O shows each result and each regulatory limit, but it does not compare them and does not publish a verdict. Whether a water system meets a standard is for its regulator to decide.
