The short version

This site demonstrates OpenH2O with two kinds of records: public records exactly as their publishers released them, and invented records that show how the accounting works. The two tables below say which records are which.

This site is a demonstration, and nothing on it has regulatory or legal standing.

{% comment %} One sentence, in the existing prose voice, no new panel and no coloured callout. It exists because this page and the per-screen labels are now two statements of the same fact, and a reader who arrives here should learn that the answer is also available where the data is; otherwise the labels are a feature only someone who already knows about them will find. {% endcomment %}

Each drinking-water section on the site also carries a label that names who published its values.

Invented sample data

Every district, agency, farm and owner in the groundwater and surface water records is invented, so the accounting names no real water district.

The drinking-water section shows real published laboratory results under the City of Merced's real name. The accounting figures on this site belong to invented organizations and say nothing about what the city pumps, owns or owes.

What Why it is invented
Agricultural wells and their construction details Private well records are not published, so the demonstration’s wells are invented.
The names of the agencies and districts The boundaries are real and the accounting inside them is invented, so each agency and district carries an invented name.
Water rights, points of diversion and deliveries The water rights are invented, and their points of diversion lie on real rivers and canals.
Owners, addresses and parcel numbers DWR’s crop map shows field shapes without owners or parcel numbers, so every owner, address and parcel number here is invented.
Crops on the irrigated fields The demonstration assigns each irrigated field a crop from a short list, so that OpenH2O can work out each field’s water balance.
Water accounts, allocations and the whole ledger These records show how OpenH2O's accounting works, and they hold no real organization's numbers.
Meters, calibration dates and account contacts The demonstration invents meters, calibration dates and account contacts so that every detail page is complete.
Managed recharge events The recharge basin shapes are real, and the volumes are invented.

How the drinking-water results are shown

The drinking-water section names a real utility and shows its real laboratory results, detections included, as the Division of Drinking Water published them.

The drinking-water section is not a compliance assessment. OpenH2O records each result and each regulatory limit as separate records; it does not compare them and does not publish a verdict. The regulator judges a water system against each standard.
