OpenH2O

Open-source water-data management for California

{% module_nouns "measurements:measurements" "accounting:deliveries" "wells:wells" "surface:surface diversions" "recharge:managed aquifer recharge" "accounting:mixed-use accounting" as data_nouns %}

OpenH2O is an open-source water-data platform for California's Groundwater Sustainability Agencies and water districts. It keeps an agency's {{ data_nouns }} in one system the agency owns. It runs on a $15-a-month server, and an AI coding assistant can deploy it from this repository.

What OpenH2O is, and who it is for

Who What they keep here
A groundwater agency or an irrigation district Its {% module_nouns "parcels:use areas" "wells:wells" "accounting:water accounts" %} on a map, every month's water on a ledger, and a water balance for each field and water year.
A ditch company or a surface-water diverter Its water rights, points of diversion and monthly diversion records, with the state's report layout written out from them.
A small public water system Its facilities, sampling points, lab results and production, carried on the state's own record layout.
An agency that watches public gauges Monitoring stations read from public feeds, each with how recently it published.
A recharge operator Its recharge sites, the events at each and their measurements.
Anyone standing it up The repository, a $15-a-month server or an office computer, and an AI coding assistant. The Getting Started page walks through the setup.

OpenH2O does not submit reports to the state. It writes the figures out in the state's layouts, and the agency files them.

What it owes to the Groundwater Accounting Platform, and what is its own

OpenH2O exists because the Groundwater Accounting Platform (GAP) proved that public agencies, a nonprofit and private firms could build and sustain shared, open-source infrastructure for water management, to a high standard.

What From the Groundwater Accounting Platform OpenH2O's own
The accounting method It gave the idea and the methodology: an agency's water is accounted month by month from satellite estimates, meters and its own records. OpenH2O reimplements that methodology independently. None of the platform's source code is copied.
The code Sitka Technology Group, now Environmental Science Associates (ESA), wrote it in its own language and stack. The Qanat community platform is the release OpenH2O's design most closely parallels. OpenH2O is written in Python with Django and GeoDjango, and it runs on PostgreSQL and PostGIS in Docker.
The scope It covers groundwater and SGMA, by design. OpenH2O covers all of water: surface water, groundwater{% if 'drinking' in enabled_modules %}, the water a public water system delivers{% endif %} and the reporting the state requires.
The license It is released under the GNU Affero General Public License, version 3. OpenH2O is released under the same license.
Who stands behind it The Environmental Defense Fund originated it in 2018 with the Rosedale-Rio Bravo Water Storage District, Sitka Technology Group engineered it, and the California Water Data Consortium opened and scaled it to agencies statewide. One maintainer and the agencies that run it stand behind OpenH2O.

OpenH2O is built on that work. OpenH2O is an independent reimplementation on an open stack, built on GAP's example.

GAP also showed where the work is: not in standing up the software, but in training, outreach and support until an agency relies on it. OpenH2O treats that work as the work.

The policy behind it

2014
SGMA signed

The Sustainable Groundwater Management Act requires roughly 200 new local agencies to bring 94 critically overdrafted basins into balance.

2016
AB 1755 passed

The Open and Transparent Water Data Act directs the state to make its water data public and interoperable.

2019
Water Data Consortium founded

An independent nonprofit forms to carry out AB 1755 and steward California's open-source water data tools.

2021
Accounting platform made available

Proven first at the Rosedale-Rio Bravo Water Storage District and in Nebraska, the Groundwater Accounting Platform is made available to California agencies through a partnership of the Consortium, the Department of Water Resources, the State Water Board and the Environmental Defense Fund.

2024
OpenET operational

Satellite evapotranspiration data becomes production-ready as a district data feed.

2025
Source code published

The Environmental Defense Fund and Environmental Science Associates publish the platform's full source code on GitHub (the Qanat project), so any agency can download and run it.

Who made it, and where the code is

What Where
Who made it Brent Vanderburgh wrote OpenH2O and maintains it. The copyright is his, and the license lets anyone run, change and share the code.
The source code The code lives at github.com/Open-H2O/openh2o under the GNU Affero General Public License v3. Anyone who modifies and hosts it must offer its users the source.
The technology OpenH2O is built from open-source components an agency can run, inspect and own outright: Django, PostGIS, MapLibre GL JS, HTMX and Docker. There is no proprietary platform and no vendor lock-in.
Third-party licenses Third-party licenses and notices lists every library OpenH2O depends on and its license.
The demonstration data What is real in this demonstration says, source by source, which records are published public record and which are invented.

Credits

Credit For
California Water Data Consortium The Consortium is the independent nonprofit founded in 2019 to carry out California's Open and Transparent Water Data Act (AB 1755). It is the neutral table where state agencies, local agencies, Tribes, academia and NGOs make the state's water data open and interoperable, and it opened the Groundwater Accounting Platform to agencies statewide.
Rosedale-Rio Bravo Water Storage District, and Nebraska's Natural Resources Districts These local agencies first ran a water-data platform in-house and proved it could be done. OpenH2O independently reimplements that approach so any agency can do the same.
Environmental Science Associates (formerly Sitka Technology Group) ESA publishes the open-source Qanat platform, which OpenH2O studied as prior art under its AGPL license. OpenH2O's design most closely parallels it; the methodology was independently reimplemented in a different language and stack, with none of their source code copied.
OpenET (NASA, USGS, the Desert Research Institute and the Environmental Defense Fund), the California Data Exchange Center (CDEC), and the USGS National Water Information System These public data services put satellite and sensor measurements within reach of any agency.
