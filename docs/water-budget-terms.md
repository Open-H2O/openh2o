# Water budget terms

This page lines up OpenH2O's own water terms with two outside references: the
California Department of Water Resources water-budget handbook, and the
budget columns an IWFM groundwater model writes out. It exists so a district
engineer or a water-budget analyst can see, term by term, which OpenH2O
number matches a state term, which is close but not the same thing, and
which has no state equivalent at all.

The handbook cited here, *Handbook for Water Budget Development: With or
Without Models*, is a **draft**. The copy DWR publishes is watermarked
"DRAFT," and no final version was found to cite instead:
https://data.cnra.ca.gov/dataset/260b8ce3-a012-48cb-bfc6-d7fe8e22db97/resource/bda1606e-34f4-44e6-8da4-8c8a615543ef/download/handbook-for-water-budget-development-with-or-without-models.pdf

The IWFM side leans on one model version for most rows: the C2VSimFG v1.5
model package, run on IWFM's root zone package version 4.11. Three of the
notes in the term-by-term table instead cite the handbook's own worked
crosswalk in its section 7, which uses a different, older sample model (an
IWFM-2015 run),
and each of those three says so: canal evaporation, canal seepage, and the
Delivered row's mention of Ag. Prime Applied Water and Ag Reused Water.
Column headers can also change in later IWFM root zone versions (4.12 and 5.0,
from the IWFM 2025 release's source code): the term-by-term table notes this on the rows the
research behind this page checked (Groundwater consumed, Delivered,
Groundwater extracted, and Water use recorded, no supply reported), and a
header elsewhere in the table has not been separately checked against
every version.

## Term by term

| OpenH2O term | Computed as | DWR handbook term and wording | IWFM column | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consumptive use | `CalculationRun.gross_et_af`, the satellite-estimated gross evapotranspiration for the field-month, called "gross actual ET" in the engine's own code (`accounting/services.py`). This is OpenH2O's own headline use figure, on the dashboard, the parcel pane, the help pages and the glossary (`DESIGN.md`'s vocabulary table). | No entry in Table 1-1. The handbook uses the idea in passing, in at least two places found: "consumptive use of applied water, also known as ETAW" (section 3.5, p. 81), and separately, section 7.2 describes a budget as reporting "the agricultural potential consumptive use of applied water and the water supply requirements." | No source defines what either column computes; by name, Ag. Actual ET (-), in the root zone budget, is the closer match to OpenH2O's own "gross actual ET" label than Ag. Potential ET is. | The handbook's found uses of the phrase tie "consumptive use" to ETAW and to a budget's overall reporting, not to a standalone gross-ET figure the way OpenH2O uses the term. |
| Net consumptive use | `CalculationRun.net_consumptive_use_af`, consumptive use minus effective rain. | ETAW. "ET of applied water (ETAW) is the amount of irrigation water needed to meet total crop ET in excess of effective precipitation." (section 3.2.1, p. 52) | Ag. ETAW | The handbook's ETAW definition, crop ET in excess of effective precipitation, matches OpenH2O's own definition of net consumptive use. No source in this crosswalk defines what IWFM's Ag. ETAW column itself computes, so the match is on the handbook's wording, not on a checked IWFM formula. |
| Effective rain | `CalculationRun.effective_precip_af`. | "Effective precipitation is the amount of precipitation stored in the root zone that is available for crop ET." (section 3.2.1, p. 52) | Ag. Effective Precipitation | The field names line up. OpenH2O computes it with the USDA-SCS method applied to GRIDMET rain (the default on Methodology Settings). No source in this crosswalk defines IWFM's own calculation for the column, so this is a naming match, not a checked numerical one. |
| Delivered | The platform's own definition is the ledger's `surface_diversion` rows (`DESIGN.md`'s vocabulary table). On a run where the efficiency setting was on, `CalculationRun.surface_delivered_af` carries that same recorded delivery as the run's own copy, before the field's own irrigation efficiency is applied; with the setting off, that field is null and the delivered figure is carried in `surface_water_af` instead (the Canal water the crop could use row). | Applied Water. "Volume of water delivered to the intake of a city water system, a factory, a farm headgate, managed wetlands, or managed aquifer recharge; it includes all sources of supply (surface water, groundwater, applied water reuse, and recycled water)." (Table 1-1, p. 9) | Ag. Deliveries (-) in the C2VSimFG v1.5 header; root zone v5.0 drops the sign notation used across this header list (research file: "no \"(+)/(-)\""). | Delivered lines up most closely with Ag. Deliveries. Separately, the handbook's own worked crosswalk in its section 7 (an older IWFM-2015 sample model, not the C2VSimFG v1.5 package this page otherwise cites) maps its broader Applied Water concept to a different pair of columns spelled "Ag. Prime Applied Water" and "Ag Reused Water" (no period, as the handbook prints it there); the C2VSimFG v1.5 file spells the second one "Ag. Reused Water," with a period. The three terms are related but not simply identical. |
| Canal water the crop could use | `CalculationRun.surface_water_af`. The on/off switch for this calculation (the efficiency setting) is a single, deployment-wide choice on the calculation plan, on by default for a newly seeded deployment; only the efficiency value itself is looked up per field. With the setting on, this is delivered water times the field's own irrigation efficiency; with that setting off, it equals the delivered figure itself. | No handbook term found in the sources reviewed. | No close IWFM column was found. | Not a direct match to anything found. OpenH2O's figure is delivered water times a fixed field efficiency, a calculation this crosswalk found no IWFM column named for. |
| Deep percolation of canal water | `surface_delivered_af` minus `surface_water_af`, summed over the period's runs (the `deep_percolation_surface_af` term in the parcel's mass balance). | Recharge of Applied Water and Precipitation. "Volume of applied water and precipitation that travels vertically through the soil/unsaturated zones and reaches the saturated zone of the aquifer (groundwater system)." (Table 1-1, p. 10) | Ag. Percolation (-) in the root zone budget; the groundwater budget's own line is Deep Percolation (+) | The handbook's definition covers both applied water and precipitation together. OpenH2O's figure tracks only the canal-water half of that: it is the gap left by the field's efficiency factor, not a measured value. |
| Groundwater consumed | `CalculationRun.final_af` on the `calculated` ledger row, the engine's estimate of the crop's well water, the remainder once rain and canal water are subtracted. | No handbook term found in the sources reviewed. | Ag. ET from Groundwater (nearest by name; the header is present in the root zone v4.11 package this page cites, and absent from root zone v5.0's header, per the release's own source code) | The header this row cites is not present in every root zone version; no source read for this crosswalk defines what the column computes in the versions where it does appear. |
| Groundwater extracted | On a field the engine estimates (has a well, no meter), `CalculationRun.gw_extracted_af`: groundwater consumed divided by the deployment's groundwater efficiency. This field is null on a metered field or a no-well field; on a metered field the meter reading is the figure instead, a separate recorded value, not this column. Either way, this is the figure the groundwater allocation is spent by. | Groundwater Extraction. "Volume of groundwater pumped (extracted) from the underlying aquifer(s) for use within the water budget zone. It does not include groundwater export, stored water extraction, and stored water export." (Table 1-1, p. 9) | Ag. Pumping (-) in the C2VSimFG v1.5 header; root zone v5.0 drops the sign notation used across this header list (research file: "no \"(+)/(-)\""). | A close match on both sides in what each counts. OpenH2O's estimated figure is the engine's own estimate of what the well pumped, not a direct measurement; only the metered case is a measurement. |
| Canal water beyond what the crop could use (over-delivery) | `CalculationRun.over_delivery_af`, the engine's own definition: "the month's canal water the crop could use beyond its net use." This is a different subtraction from deep percolation of canal water above it: that row is delivered water minus the efficiency-adjusted amount the crop could use; this row is the efficiency-adjusted amount plus rain, minus what the crop actually needed, with genuine rainfall on its own set aside first. | No handbook term found in the sources reviewed. | No IWFM column found in the sources reviewed. | OpenH2O records the amount, and separately, what the deployment's setting does with it. |
| Canal evaporation | A fixed share of the diverted volume (`PointOfDiversion.evaporation_fraction`), recorded on the point of diversion, the location on the stream where the water is taken. | Conveyance Evaporation. "Volume of water evaporated into the atmosphere from conveyance facilities, other than streams, during water delivery." (Table 1-1, p. 9) | Non-Recoverable Loss (the handbook's own section 7.4.4, crosswalking an older IWFM-2015 sample model, not the C2VSimFG v1.5 package this page otherwise cites) | Canal losses are recorded, not yet applied, covers this row below the table: this figure is recorded but not yet read by the calculation. |
| Canal seepage | A fixed share of the diverted volume (`PointOfDiversion.seepage_fraction`), recorded on the point of diversion, the location on the stream where the water is taken. | Conveyance Seepage. "Volume of water recharged to the groundwater system from the conveyance facilities, other than streams, during water delivery." (Table 1-1, p. 9) | Recoverable Loss (the handbook's own section 7.4.5, crosswalking the same older IWFM-2015 sample model) | Canal losses are recorded, not yet applied, covers this row below the table: this figure is recorded but not yet read by the calculation. |
| Spill | A fixed share of the diverted volume (`PointOfDiversion.spill_fraction`), recorded on the point of diversion, the location on the stream where the water is taken. | No term in the handbook. The word does not appear in the document. | No term found in the IWFM sources reviewed. | Canal losses are recorded, not yet applied, covers this row below the table: this figure is recorded but not yet read by the calculation. |
| Return flow | `DiversionRecord.returned_af`, the part of a diversion an agency states was returned to the stream rather than retained (retained water is delivered or taken to storage). | Return Flow. "Volume of applied water that is not consumptively used and flows to the surface water system. It includes treated wastewater discharges to the surface water system." (Table 1-1, p. 10) | Ag. Net Return Flow | Not a full match. The handbook's Return Flow is specifically applied water that went unused; OpenH2O's field can also record a full-volume, non-consumptive or hydropower passthrough, water that was never applied at all, so the two do not always mean the same volume. |
| Water use recorded, no supply reported | Sum of `CalculationRun.unmet_demand_af` for a field where `residual_disposition` is `unmet_demand`: the part of the field's crop water use left over, after rain and canal water are subtracted, that no supply on record explains. | No handbook term found in the sources reviewed. | Ag. Shortage (=) in the C2VSimFG v1.5 header (nearest by name); v4.12's Land and Water Use Budget drops the Ag. Inflow as Surface Runoff (-) column entirely, and root zone v5.0 drops the sign notation used across this header list (research file: "no \"(+)/(-)\""). | Not the same measurement. Ag. Shortage is the result marked "(=)" in the header row, after the deductions marked "(-)" (Ag. Pumping, Ag. Deliveries, Ag. Inflow as Surface Runoff) are taken from Ag. Supply Requirement. The signs show the arithmetic, but no source read for this crosswalk defines what any of those columns compute. OpenH2O's figure is the leftover of the satellite ET estimate less estimated effective rain less the efficiency-adjusted canal delivery, a different calculation reaching a similar idea. It states what the record shows and nothing about the grower. |

## Canal losses are recorded, not yet applied

Canal evaporation, canal seepage and spill are each recorded on the point
of diversion, the location on the stream where the water is taken. Each is
a fixed share of the diverted volume, set by the agency or, where the
agency has not stated one, left at this platform's own default (zero for
seepage and spill, since guessing a district's real figure would be worse
than saying nothing; a small default for evaporation). The calculation
engine that turns a field's satellite water use into a groundwater and
surface-water balance does not read those three figures yet. They sit on
the point-of-diversion record and are not yet part of the calculation.
